## Current agreed design

1. ServiceChain owns admitted commands in `serviceIndex` order, each original `outcome` and complete pending external requests. ServiceVersionRepo evaluates synchronous programs and owns resources, `materializationHead` and complete pending ServiceVersionChain publication. ServiceVersionChain owns immutable evaluated-command history. A materialization owner retains no permanent command/execution history.
2. ServiceVersionRepo commits resource changes or business refusal, its checkpoint and a complete evaluated command together. `IEvaluatedServiceCommand` retains full admitted input, canonical `id`, original `serviceVersion`, `serviceIndex`, `outcome: { result, finalizedAt }`, `dispositionChainHash` and one ordered model/command/external `operations` union. Every member retains its authored sparse `operationIndex`. The bound VersionRepo/VersionChain identifies the evaluating release.
3. The same complete value returns through original finalization and publishes to ServiceVersionChain. Duplicate original execution recovers exact pending publication or immutable history without reevaluation, fresh external IDs or resampled time. A committed Repo checkpoint ahead of ServiceChain's finalized prefix must survive cold startup. Publication acknowledgement and ServiceChain's original receipt are independent durable boundaries; neither a reverse confirmation protocol nor a separate `serviceChainOutbox` exists.
4. ServiceChain's first synchronous original-receipt transaction sets `commands.outcome` and inserts six-field pending external invocations: unique `externalCommandId`, exact `externalContractName`/`externalContractVersion`, frozen JSON `payload`, full `origin` and emitting `serviceVersion`. Pending parent/index lineage and its composite index are absent. Exact retained-outcome retries return before request insertion; full evaluated-command equality belongs to ServiceVersionChain.
5. ServiceChain alone runs `external.request({ payload })`, after original receipt. It resolves exact frozen release/contract identity without current-release fallback. Requests run outside SQL and command execution permits. Independent IDs remain reserved through request finalizers, observation recording and recovery reconciliation. Requests receive no database or implicit provider idempotency key.
6. First observation re-reads pending work, admits its stable-ID external command at the next `serviceIndex` and deletes the request in one synchronous transaction. Failed admission/deletion commits neither fact and consumes no position. Frozen payload, exact release/contract, full Actor/Agent/System Origin and all-null `nodeId`/`sessionName`/`pushIndex` survive. Missing pending work makes late callbacks no-ops.
7. Observation admission is distinct from continuation execution and retained `outcome`. The admitted observation executes through ordinary original delivery in its pinned ServiceVersionRepo. Its synchronous continuation may interpret Success or terminal external Failure, update resources or emit further operations. Failure before its execution commit preserves the admitted observation for recovery; a committed observation must not cause another provider request.
8. Process loss before durable observation admission may repeat provider effects. Authored provider idempotency/reconciliation remains necessary. Recovery alarms precede producer commits; activation and original completion reconcile pending work, including an empty original drain after commit-before-hook loss.
9. Replay and projections apply model members only; they never schedule external requests. Preserve exact declarations, full historical authority, resource/history separation and synchronous transaction boundaries. Fixed-schema changes require empty affected storage. No reset of existing stores, migration, compatibility alias/fallback, deployment or publication is authorized.
